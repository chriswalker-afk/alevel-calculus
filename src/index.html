A level Calculus · 8MA0 / 9MA0

Understand it. Remember it. Practise it. Explain it. Apply it.

This site is designed to take you from first understanding a calculus idea to using it confidently in unfamiliar problems. Each topic is organised as a learning journey rather than a list of notes: explore the idea, retrieve the key facts, practise the standard methods, explain the mathematics, then apply it.

You do not have to use every section every time. If a topic is already secure, move quickly to the practice you need. If something breaks down, use Need a reminder? to jump back to the right explanation, fact or practice activity.

First steps

A good way to use the site

For a new topic, work from understanding towards independence. The order is deliberate, but you can move backwards whenever you need to.

  1. 1 Choose your topic

    Use the Topics panel to find the part of the course you are studying. The pathway separates Year 12 content from the additional Year 13 material.

  2. 2 Start with Understand

    Read the Topic goals, then work through the explanations, diagrams and interactives until the main ideas make sense.

  3. 3 Make the facts retrievable

    Use Memorise for notation, rules, definitions and key facts. Flashcards, games and review activities are there to make recall quick.

  4. 4 Move through AO1 → AO2 → AO3

    First practise the technique, then reasoning and explanation, then harder applications and modelling.

  5. 5 Use feedback to loop back

    Check answers, reveal hints when useful and follow targeted reminder links instead of simply repeating the same question.

The five learning modes

What each section is for

Most topics use the same five-part structure. Some foundation or review topics deliberately use fewer modes when another section would add no value.

Understand · Explore

Build the idea

Concept explanations, worked examples, visual models and interactive diagrams. Use this when the mathematics is new or when a method has stopped making sense.

Ask: “Do I understand why this works?”
Memorise · Recall

Make key knowledge available

Definitions, notation, standard results and method cues are turned into active-recall tasks, flashcards, games and mixed review.

Ask: “Can I recall this without looking it up?”
AO1 · Practise

Use and apply standard techniques

AO1 is about carrying out familiar mathematical procedures accurately and efficiently: algebra, differentiation, integration and other standard methods.

Ask: “Can I execute the method correctly?”
AO2 · Explain

Reason, interpret and communicate

AO2 asks you to justify choices, interpret results, connect representations, diagnose errors and communicate mathematical reasoning clearly.

Ask: “Can I explain or justify the mathematics?”
AO3 · Apply

Solve problems in context

AO3 is about choosing and connecting mathematics in less familiar problems, including modelling and problems set in mathematical or real-world contexts.

Ask: “Can I decide what mathematics to use?”

Review sections mix topics so you must recognise the structure yourself. They are designed for consolidation once individual topics are reasonably secure.
